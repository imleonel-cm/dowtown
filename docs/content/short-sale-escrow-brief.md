# Short Sale Escrow — content brief

## Audience and purpose

This page is for sellers, buyers, agents, lenders or servicers, title contacts, and other participants who want to discuss escrow coordination for a possible or approved short sale. It explains the distinction between closing coordination and short sale approval without presenting Downtown as a negotiator, lender, legal adviser, tax adviser, credit adviser, or nationwide provider.

## Content implemented

- A plain-language definition of a short sale and a direct explanation of escrow's role.
- Clear boundaries for lender/servicer approval, sale negotiation, title/lien matters, and professional advice.
- Four transaction variables: written approvals, title and liens, dates and changes, and transaction-specific documentation.
- A four-part preparation path and a non-mandatory orientation list for an initial conversation.
- Five short-sale-specific FAQs, including cautious guidance on deficiency balances. Preparation information remains in the dedicated preparation and orientation sections instead of being repeated in the FAQ.
- A limited sources section and telephone/localized Home-contact paths.

## Official sources and scope

### Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

[What is a short sale?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-short-sale-en-290/)

Used for the general definition, the need for lender or servicer agreement, the possibility of a deficiency, the importance of a written waiver, and the fact that state law can matter. It does not describe Downtown's services or determine the result of a specific transaction.

[Avoid foreclosure — Request approval for a short sale](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/housing/housing-insecurity/help-for-homeowners/avoid-foreclosure/)

Used for general context about mortgage-servicer approval, other mortgages, loss-mitigation review, and understanding the terms of the specific short sale. The page does not convert CFPB consumer guidance into Downtown procedures.

### U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development

[FHA's Loss Mitigation Program](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/fha-loss-mitigation)

Used as a clearly labeled program-specific example: FHA describes a Pre-Foreclosure Sale as a home-disposition option with its own eligibility and conditions. FHA requirements are not presented as rules for every loan or short sale.

Sources were rechecked on October 27, 2026. They are independent government references, not affiliations, certifications, endorsements, or operational documentation for Downtown.
