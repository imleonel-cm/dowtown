# Manufactured & Mobile Home Escrow — content brief

## Audience and intent

This page is for owners, buyers, sellers, agents, lenders, and other transaction participants who want to discuss escrow involving a manufactured or mobile home. It should help a visitor identify useful context for an initial conversation without presenting a universal opening checklist, guaranteed workflow, legal conclusion, lending guide, or promise that Downtown can serve every jurisdiction.

The page has a broad commercial orientation. Property location is requested because classification, records, agencies, and requirements can vary by jurisdiction; the Santa Ana office address remains in the established contact and footer contexts.

## Approved content to implement

- A direct explanation of the specialty and who may find it relevant.
- Four transaction variables: whether land is included, available title or registration information, property location, and the professionals involved.
- Clear boundaries between escrow coordination and real estate representation, lending or loan approval, title insurance, and legal or tax advice.
- A four-part consultation-preparation path that is explicitly not an internal workflow or guaranteed timeline.
- An orientation list of information that may be useful for a first conversation, not mandatory documents.
- Five specialty-specific FAQs covering land, state differences, out-of-state inquiries, lending, and formal file opening. Initial information is handled in the preparation and orientation sections instead of being repeated in the FAQ.
- A discreet related-sources section that explains the limited scope of each source.
- Phone and localized Home-contact paths that distinguish a conversation from formally opening an escrow file.

## Sources and supported statements

### Fannie Mae

[Titling manufactured homes as real property](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/originating-underwriting/titling-manufactured-homes-real-property)

Used only to support the general statement that manufactured-home titling approaches and requirements can vary by state and that legal counsel and title professionals may be relevant. The source describes manufactured housing within Fannie Mae’s mortgage eligibility and lender-compliance framework. Its certificate, affixture, mortgage, and title-insurance discussion is not treated as a universal transaction process or as a description of Downtown’s work.

### California Department of Housing and Community Development

[Buy, Sell, or No-Sale Transfer Instructions](https://www.hcd.ca.gov/mmh/registration-titling/transfer)

Used only to support the statement that California has specific registration and titling instructions and that the applicable records or forms can depend on the transfer and record context. HCD’s forms and procedures are California-specific and are not presented as national requirements.
