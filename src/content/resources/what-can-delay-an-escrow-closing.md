Closing day is getting closer. Everyone expects the transaction to be ready.

Then a document is still missing. An issue hasn’t been cleared. Funding isn’t ready when expected.

Suddenly, a closing that felt right on track needs more time.

The frustrating part? Some closing delays don’t begin on closing day. They start earlier in the transaction and become more noticeable when there’s less time left to resolve them.

So, what can delay an escrow closing?

While every transaction is different, these are three issues worth watching as you get closer to closing.

## 1. A Document or Signature Is Still Missing

A real estate transaction involves information and documents coming from multiple parties. Escrow helps coordinate the closing process, but that process can’t always move forward when a required item is incomplete or still outstanding.

Sometimes it’s something simple: a document needs a signature, information needs to be corrected, or an item requested earlier still hasn’t been returned.

The closer you get to closing, the less room there may be to resolve those loose ends without affecting timing.

### What can help?

Pay attention to requests from your escrow team, lender, agent, and other professionals involved in the transaction. If something needs your signature or additional information, handling it promptly can keep a small pending item from becoming a bigger problem later.

And if you’re not sure what’s still outstanding, ask.

A simple question can be useful:

> “Is there anything you’re still waiting on from me?”

## 2. An Issue Still Needs to Be Cleared

Sometimes everyone has submitted their paperwork, but there is still an issue that needs to be resolved before the transaction can move forward.

Depending on the transaction, that could involve a title-related matter, a lender requirement, updated information, or another condition that involves one or more parties.

This is where communication matters.

Not every issue is controlled by escrow, and not every delay can be prevented by escrow. Buyers, sellers, agents, lenders, title professionals and other parties may all have responsibilities during the transaction.

What escrow can do is help coordinate the pieces that fall within its role and keep the appropriate parties informed as the file moves toward closing.

That distinction matters.

A good closing process isn’t about pretending that nothing unexpected will happen. It’s about knowing what is still pending and addressing it before everyone is waiting on closing day.

## 3. Funding Isn’t Ready When Expected

For transactions involving financing, reaching the final stages of the loan process is a major milestone.

But “clear to close” doesn’t mean the transaction has already closed.

There are still final steps that may need to happen between loan approval and the completion of the transaction.

The lender may need final signed documents or other required items before funds are released. Escrow then works with the appropriate parties on the remaining closing steps.

If funding doesn’t arrive when expected, the closing timeline may need to move.

That’s why buyers shouldn’t treat an estimated closing date as confirmation that everything is already complete.

If you’re getting close to closing, it’s reasonable to ask:

Has everything needed for the next step been received?

That question is much more useful than simply assuming the transaction is ready because the scheduled date is approaching.

## Can Escrow Prevent Every Closing Delay?

No.

And an escrow company shouldn’t make that promise.

Escrow is an important part of the closing process, but many pieces of a real estate transaction involve decisions, documents and requirements from other parties.

What makes a difference is clear coordination before the final day.

Knowing what has been received, what is still pending and who is responsible for the next step can make the process easier to understand and can help identify issues that need attention.

For buyers and sellers going through escrow in California, communication becomes especially important as the expected closing date approaches.

Don’t be afraid to ask what happens next.

## Before Closing Day, Ask These Questions

You don’t need to understand every document in the transaction to stay involved in your closing.

Start with a few practical questions:

- Is anything still needed from me?
- Are there documents that still need to be signed or returned?
- Is there anything currently pending that could affect the expected closing date?
- What happens next on the escrow side of the transaction?
- Who should I contact if I have a question before closing?

The goal isn’t to worry about everything that could go wrong.

It’s to know where your transaction stands.
